Anxious People

by Fredrik Backman

✍ Shweta Suryavanshi 📅 September 20, 2026 ⏱ 11 min read
Author Fredrik Backman
Language Swedish (translated to English)
Published 2019 (Swedish), 2020 (English Translation)
Genre Contemporary Fiction, Humor, Literary Drama
Setting Stockholm, Sweden — A Single Day
Themes Mental Health, Connection, Loneliness, Hope, Human Dignity, Vulnerability

Summary

Anxious People begins with a failed bank robbery and spirals into something far more human and touching—a story about eight strangers brought together by circumstance on a chaotic apartment viewing in Stockholm. An anxious middle-aged man attempts to rob a bank without a plan, holds eight apartment hunters hostage, and then quietly disappears, leaving behind a puzzle and a revelation: these eight people, thrown together by coincidence, end up saving each other, though none of them quite realizes it at first.

Through interconnected narratives, we glimpse into the lives of a desperate couple trying to sell their apartment, a lonely old woman on the verge of giving up, a young boy trying to be the man his family needs, a woman navigating burnout, and others wrestling with invisible struggles. Backman's genius is in showing how human connection can bloom in the most unexpected moments, how grace isn't something earned but something shared between people brave enough to be broken in front of each other.

The Radical Act of Being Seen

What moved me most profoundly was Backman's ability to dignify anxiety, depression, and failure without sentimentalizing them. Each character carries a specific weight—financial pressure, grief, burnout, isolation—yet none of them are defined solely by their pain. Instead, they are complex, often funny, occasionally infuriating, always recognizably human. The book doesn't pretend that acknowledging pain solves it, but it suggests something equally powerful: that being witnessed in your struggle, that having someone sit with you in the mess, changes something fundamental.

There's a scene late in the novel that undoes you—not with dramatic revelation but with quiet recognition. These eight people, who were strangers hours before, have become a kind of family through nothing more than honest conversation and shared vulnerability. It's not a fix-it narrative; it's a recognition that sometimes the greatest gift we can offer another person isn't solutions but presence.

"What a lot of people don't understand about people like him is that we don't want to be saved. We just want to be looked at—really looked at—without anyone's eyes sliding away." — Fredrik Backman, Anxious People

What Lingers

I finished this book laughing and then crying in my own living room, which I suppose is the entire point. Backman writes with such warmth and humor about the deepest struggles of ordinary life. There's no grand tragedy here, no dramatic arc of redemption—just very human people getting tired, getting scared, reaching toward each other in the dark.

In a world that often demands we present polished, competent versions of ourselves, Anxious People feels like permission to be messy, uncertain, struggling. It's a love letter to everyone who's felt like too much and not enough at the same time. The novel doesn't promise that connection will fix everything, but it does suggest that being genuinely seen by another person—flaws and all—is itself a form of grace. That's a message our anxious, isolated world desperately needs to hear.
